Brings AI into engineering through two controlled paths. The first is
AI-augmented delivery: repeatable support for analysis, architecture, coding,

governance: selecting viable use cases, defining measurable acceptance criteria

Works at the boundary between business priorities and implementation reality.
Makes trade-offs explicit through architecture decisions, delivery controls and
operational evidence, avoiding both unmanaged AI experimentation and
technology-first programmes without a measurable outcome.

## AI direction — two governance responsibilities

### 1. Govern AI-augmented software delivery

- Defines where AI can accelerate the SDLC and where deterministic automation or
  human judgement must remain authoritative.
- Establishes reusable engineering workflows for requirements analysis,
  architecture review, code generation, test design, documentation and
  merge-request analysis.
- Requires generated changes to pass compilation, tests, security scanning,
  architectural checks and human review.
- Governs repository context, approved models, data boundaries, prompt assets
  and cost visibility to avoid uncontrolled shadow-AI usage.
- Measures the effect of AI adoption through delivery and quality baselines
  rather than anecdotal productivity claims.

### 2. Govern AI capabilities released to users and clients

- Starts with the business decision, baseline and failure cost; rejects AI use
  cases where deterministic software is safer or less expensive.
- Requires golden datasets and evaluation thresholds for retrieval, generation
  and structured-output quality.
- Establishes controls for prompt injection, PII leakage, access boundaries,
  auditability and human approval.
- Treats model providers as external dependencies: timeout, retry, circuit
  breaker, fallback and provider abstraction are explicit architecture choices.
- Requires production telemetry covering latency, errors, token consumption,
  cost, model selection and quality regressions.

## Differentiators — proposed copy

- **Technical manager who understands the implementation:** can challenge an
  architecture, review code and reason about production failure modes.
- **AI-native, not AI-first:** introduces AI only where evidence supports the
  additional complexity and operating cost.
- **Governance without theatre:** decisions become ADRs, tests, policies,
  dashboards and accountable owners.
- **Connects SDLC AI and product AI:** governs both developer acceleration and
  AI capabilities exposed through business software.
