## Professional summary — proposed copy

Software and Architecture Manager with a developer-first background and
end-to-end ownership across architecture, delivery quality, operational
reliability and technical governance. Leads complex modernisation programmes
while remaining close enough to the code to challenge designs, review critical
changes and unblock teams.

Uses AI on two fronts that must stay under control. First: AI-augmented
delivery — repeatable support for analysis, architecture, coding, testing,
review and incident investigation. Second: applied AI governance — selecting
viable product use cases and requiring evaluation, security, cost visibility,
observability and fallback behaviour before an AI capability is scaled.

Operates at the boundary between business priorities and implementation
reality. Makes trade-offs explicit through architecture decisions, delivery
controls and operating evidence, avoiding both unmanaged AI experiments and
technology-first programmes without a measurable outcome.

## AI direction — two governance responsibilities

### 1. Govern AI-augmented software delivery

- Defines where AI accelerates the SDLC and where deterministic automation or
  human judgement must remain authoritative.
- Establishes reusable workflows for requirements analysis, architecture
  review, code generation, test design, documentation and change analysis.
- Requires AI-assisted changes to pass compilation, tests, security scanning,
  architectural checks and human review.
- Governs approved tools, context boundaries, prompt assets and cost visibility
  to reduce uncontrolled shadow-AI usage.
- Tracks adoption through delivery and quality signals rather than anecdotal
  productivity stories.

### 2. Govern AI capabilities for users and clients

- Starts from the business decision, baseline and failure cost; rejects AI where
  deterministic software is safer or less expensive.
- Requires evaluation criteria for retrieval, generation and structured outputs.
- Establishes controls for sensitive data, access boundaries, auditability and
  human approval.
- Treats model providers as external dependencies with timeout, retry, circuit
  breaker, fallback and abstraction — without oversharing platform internals.
- Expects telemetry for latency, errors, cost, model selection and quality
  regressions before scaling.

## Differentiators — proposed copy

- **Technical manager who still understands implementation:** can challenge an
  architecture, review code and reason about failure modes.
- **AI-native, not AI-first:** introduces AI only where evidence justifies the
  added complexity and operating cost.
- **Governance without theatre:** decisions become ADRs, tests, policies,
  dashboards and accountable owners.
- **Connects SDLC AI and product AI:** governs both developer acceleration and
  AI capabilities exposed through business software.
